## View radius

Read-only sources: `atrinik/classic@b651c55fd0fe`, `atrinik/client@29cb5f66bc06`, `atrinik/server@7f80ae5f5792`.

- Classic server: `MAP_CLIENT_X 32` / `MAP_CLIENT_Y 32`, the largest map view a client may request (`server/src/include/config.h:60-61`). The setup handler accepts any requested size up to that (`server/src/socket/request.c:154-161`), and `mapx_2 = mapx / 2` (`server/src/socket/init.c:77`). The map update sends x from `pl->x - mapx_2` to `pl->x + (mapx + 1) / 2 - 1` and likewise for y (`server/src/socket/request.c:1305-1311`): **16 tiles** west and north, 15 east and south at size 32.
- Classic client: the user-visible look size is at most `MAP_LOOK_SIZE_MAX 17`, and the client adds `MAP_RENDER_OVERSCAN 2` tiles on each edge, so the size on the wire is at most 21 (`client/src/include/map.h:68-83`). The client refuses anything larger from the server (`client/src/client/commands.c:139-140`). That is **10 tiles** each way.
- Replacement stack: neither `atrinik/client@29cb5f66bc06` nor `atrinik/server@7f80ae5f5792` defines a view radius yet.
- **The plan uses 16 tiles**, the server's bound. Comparison run with the shipped client's 10 tiles (`view_radius_sensitivity` in the JSON): 65 stairs, 48 stairwell, 19 portal; 38 stairwells; groups that would sit elsewhere: `mine_unique_a`, `ellamin`, `dark_cave`.
- Other layers: classic also streams the linked levels from 6 below to 6 above (`MAP2_MAX_DEPTH 6`, `libatrinik/socket.h:87-89`), but levels hidden from the camera are pruned before they are sent (`server/src/server/los.c:737`); a level below the player is hidden by the floor of the player's level (`server/src/server/los.c:393-400`). The gap rule therefore applies per layer.

## How to read this plan

- **Plane coordinates**: overworld tile (x, y) of `world_X_Y[_Z]` is plane (24X+x, 24Y+y). Its layer is Z unless the relayering moved it; every layer in this plan is the final one, and `occupied_rows_by_layer` in the JSON gives each map's tiles per final layer. Overworld plane extent: x -240..479, y 0..2159.
- **Group-local layout**: each member map gets a local origin by walking tile links from the layout root (N/S/E/W/diagonal neighbours offset by the neighbour's or the map's own width/height exactly like the server's `get_map_from_coord()`, up/down = local layer +1/-1). Plane tile = local origin + (x, y) + (x_offset, y_offset); plane layer = local layer + layer_offset.
- **Exit resolution** mirrors the server: `slaying` path relative to the map directory or absolute; `hp/sp` target (-1/-1 = the map's enter_x/enter_y); `last_heal` 1..10 = tiled exit through that tile link (with the `xrays` direction offset); coordinates outside the target map walk its tile links.
- **Layer choice**: the main connection fixes the ideal layer: one layer below its source for down-exits, one above for up-exits. Neutral exits take the group's orientation (exit semantics of its connections, then name keywords, then the default: below). Every directional exit to an already placed thing is also a hard bound.
- **Search**: if the ideal spot breaks the gap rule, the next 6 deeper (or higher) layers are tried at the same x/y, keeping the main connection aligned so it becomes a stairwell; a spot whose footprint fits but whose stairwell is blocked is taken next; only then are horizontal shifts up to 96 tiles tried. Groups are placed in reach order, then by id, after the relayering.
- **becomes**: `stairs` = the two ends are exactly one layer and at most 1 tile apart; `stairwell` = 2 or more layers apart and aligned (SW, SR) or at most one tile per layer apart (SL); `portal` = still a teleport, with `portal_reason` in the JSON.

## Relayering (applied)

Owner decision 2026-10-18: fix the conflicts, then relayer. JSON key `relayering`; every tile of the overworld is on its final layer everywhere else in the plan. The before/after images are `relayer_<n>.png` ([Layer images](#layer-images)).

### Rule

- **Height**: a tile's height H is its support height, the highest floor z over all sub-layers, the height upper levels stand on in classic (`map_space_support_height()`, `server/src/socket/request.c:1012-1022`). Version 1's proposal read only sub-layer 0 floors and missed the raised walkways and wall-walks on sub-layers 1 and 2: 1,960 overworld tiles reach H >= 50 only through such a floor. In all, 35,102 overworld floor tiles have H >= 50 (the proposal counted 33,146 on placed maps).
- **Split**: H = 50 q + rest with q = H div 50 for H >= 50; a relayered tile moves q layers up and keeps rest (0..49) as its tile height. A tile carrying a low floor on sub-layer 0 under a raised floor on a higher sub-layer (a bridge over ground) is split: the raised part moves, the ground part stays (200 tiles).
- **Regions**: 8-connected tiles of one layer with the same q. A region of at least 24 tiles is relayered, a smaller one only when it touches a relayered region (another step of the same slope); the rest keep z as tile height.
- **Units**: 8-connected occupied tiles of one layer. The surface (layer 0) is never a unit: only its regions move.
- **Conflicts first**, repeated until none is left: in every column the tiles keep their vertical order, so a storey above ground that rises moves up with it; a cave whose raised floor would reach the tile above it, or the surface layer, moves down instead. A cave moves down as a whole (its raised regions land back on their layer and its slopes stay intact) when that pushes nothing below it and, compared with lowering only the raised regions in place, changes no more stairs and makes no more steep steps, and fewer of at least one; otherwise, ties included, only the raised regions are lowered in place (they keep their layer and their floor keeps z - 50 q).
- **Walkable**: two neighbouring tiles with floors, neither holding a `no_pass` object, less than 25 z apart. That is a step the classic server counts as no fall: it deals fall damage for round(dz / 50) floors (`server/src/server/move.c:151`). A walkable edge whose tiles end on different layers is **cut**; every cut is flagged and joined by stairs ([Cut walkable slopes](#cut-walkable-slopes)).

### View gap and void rules

- **Void**: every moved tile lands on a void tile; no tile is claimed twice (0 overlaps).
- **View gap**: placement runs after the relayering, so every placed group keeps the 16-tile gap from the relayered overworld (re-checked by `verify_plan.py`). Raised ground and storeys stay above ground, where classic draws the levels together; caves stay below layer 0, so no cave becomes visible from the surface.
- **Steep steps** (walkable edges that stay on one layer but become a fall): world_7_47_-1 (0,17) / world_7_47_-1 (0,18) on L-1: z 50/48 -> 0/48; world_7_47_-1 (0,17) / world_7_47_-1 (1,18) on L-1: z 50/48 -> 0/48; world_7_47_-1 (0,18) / world_7_47_-1 (1,17) on L-1: z 48/50 -> 48/0; world_7_47_-1 (1,17) / world_7_47_-1 (1,18) on L-1: z 50/48 -> 0/48.
- **New contacts** (tiles that become neighbours on a layer although they were on different layers): world_8_46 (13,13) / world_8_46_1 (14,14) on L1 (not walkable: a wall or no floor between them); world_8_46 (14,12) / world_8_46_1 (15,13) on L1 (not walkable: a wall or no floor between them).

## Stairwells

Each row is one stairwell (`stairwells[]` in the JSON): one stair tile per layer from the top end to the bottom end. Top and bottom are the existing exit objects; the intermediate tiles are new decorations that borrow the appearance of the family's `*_down` archetype (or, when no exit belongs to a stair family, of the archetype the owner chose for its exits: `invis_exit` -> `stairs_stone_down`; `well_bucket` -> `ladder_down`; `well_stone` -> `ladder_down`), are not exits and not walkable, so the stairwell stays one exit. **Every stairwell is drawn only on the player's layer** (`segments_drawn_only_on_player_layer`), so its **view conflicts** (intermediate tiles within 16 tiles of an unrelated area on that layer) are listed but not seen. The chain search also avoids tiles within 1 tile of a related area (the contact margin); **contacts** it cannot avoid are listed. A stairwell that replaces a legacy one-way exit is used only that way (**use**: down or up); its far end is a landing without an exit. ## Stairwell follow-up (engine and format)

Decided semantics: a stairwell is ONE exit for use and pathfinding, in classic and in other games. One use moves the player from one end to the other; pathfinding sees one edge; editors and players see a long staircase through the layers. **New (2026-10-18): clients draw a stairwell's segments only on the layer the player is on**, and stairwells may slope one tile per layer. Not implemented here; needed before the migration can emit stairwells:

- **Classic server** (`atrinik/classic@b651c55fd0fe`):
  - Tiled stairs move exactly one linked level: `last_heal` 9/10 resolve through one `tile_path` (`server/src/types/exit.c:300-320`), and landing picks a floor by sub-layer direction (`server/src/server/object.c:4703`). A stairwell end needs an explicit destination at the other end (layer included), not a tiled link.
  - The intermediate stair objects must not be exits (no `P_IS_EXIT`) and must not be walkable or applicable, or each would be a separate edge or a shortcut. They are decorations that belong to one stairwell record.
  - Pathfinding already treats an exit as one edge to `exit_get_destination()` (`server/src/server/pathfinder.c:1034-1078`, `server/src/types/exit.c:377`); a stairwell must resolve to its far end there, never to an intermediate layer.
  - **Off-layer hiding**: the map update streams other levels per cell and depth (`server/src/socket/request.c:1305-1311`, camera classification in `server/src/server/los.c:386-415`). The server must skip a stairwell's intermediate objects on every depth except the player's own (depth 0), so a client never receives a segment on another layer.
  - The plane loader must create the stairwell from the plan's record and validate it (continuity of at most one tile per layer, void intermediate tiles, and each end's exits target the other end; a one-way stairwell, `direction` down or up, has exits only at the end it starts from and a landing at the other).
- **Classic client** (`atrinik/classic@b651c55fd0fe`):
  - Linked levels are projected 46 px apart (`client/src/gui/widgets/map.c:1307`, applied at `:4712-4713`) and up to 6 levels each way are streamed (`libatrinik/socket.h:87-89`).
  - **Off-layer hiding**: when the server does not filter, the client must not draw a stairwell segment whose depth is not 0: `map_should_draw()` (`client/src/gui/widgets/map.c:4703`) already decides per cell and depth whether a tile is drawn, but the stairwell needs a marker in the map protocol for it to know which objects are segments.
  - The exit cue (`MAP2_FLAG2_EXIT`, `server/src/socket/request.c:1831`; `map_render_command_draw_exit()`, `client/src/gui/widgets/map.c:7311`) should mark the stairwell's ends as one exit, not each intermediate tile.
- **Tilewright world format** (`seraphinastudios/tilewright-world@6fe670f47630`):
  - Today an exit is an object with a `portal` to `(plane, layer, at)` (`docs/spec/FORMAT.md:210`, example at `:176`). There is no way to say that objects on several layers form one exit.
  - Needed: a stairwell record per plane (id, ordered tiles `(layer, x, y, proto)` from top to bottom, the two end objects, a direction (both, down, up), a `drawn_only_on_player_layer` flag that defaults to true), validation rules (consecutive tiles one layer and at most one tile apart, so sloped stairwells are valid; every end portal targets the other end, and a one-way stairwell has portals only at its start; the intermediate objects are not portals), a pack table for it, and editor support to show and edit the stairwell as one object.
  - Relayering output fits the format as it is: per-tile `height` grids (`docs/spec/FORMAT.md:194-195`) take the remaining heights, split bridge tiles keep their raised floor as an object with its `sub_layer` (`:196-197`), and the new seam stairs are ordinary objects with a `portal`.

## Layer-height evidence

Read-only source: `atrinik/classic@b651c55fd0fe`.

- Floor z is a screen-pixel height. The server sends an object's `z` as its height (`server/src/socket/request.c:1718`, `:1965`); the client reads it (`client/src/client/commands.c:1335`) and draws the tile that many pixels higher (`client/src/gui/widgets/map.c:8499`). Negative floor heights draw as 0 (`get_top_floor_height()`, `client/src/gui/widgets/map.c:3386-3391`).
- One linked level is drawn **46 px** higher (`MAP_LEVEL_PIXEL_HEIGHT`, `client/src/gui/widgets/map.c:1307`, applied at `:4712`), on top of the support height under it, the highest floor over all sub-layers (`map_space_support_height()`, `server/src/socket/request.c:1012-1022`; `client/src/gui/widgets/map.c:4713`).
- Double wall rendering: `draw_double` objects (`server/src/socket/request.c:1742`) are drawn twice, the second copy **22 px** higher (`client/src/gui/widgets/map.c:4309`, `:7142`, `:7320`). The double-drawn 48x47 wall sprites rise 47 - 23 = 24 px, and doubled 24 + 22 = 46 px: exactly one level.
- `HEIGHT_MAX_RENDER 50` (`client/src/include/map.h:319`): tiles more than 50 px above or below the player are hidden in fog of war and on height-difference maps.
- The server counts a fall of z 50 as one floor: moving to a lower floor deals fall damage for round((z_from - z_to) / 50) floors (`server/src/server/move.c:151`). A step under 25 is no fall, which is the walkable threshold the relayering uses.
- A walking player cannot enter a tile whose floor terrain bits it lacks (`blocked()`, `server/src/server/map.c:697`; floor `terrain_type` folded in at `:2189`; `TERRAIN_AIRBREATH 1`, `TERRAIN_WATERWALK 2` in `server/src/include/define.h:678-680`). The wired-exit verdicts use this.

## Reproducing

```sh
cd migration/placement-plan
python3 -I scripts/build_plan.py --content /home/zoey/dev/worktrees/content-plan-v2-input --out .
python3 -I scripts/verify_plan.py placement-plan.json --content /home/zoey/dev/worktrees/content-plan-v2-input
python3 -I scripts/test_placement_plan.py
python3 -I scripts/build_plan.py --content /home/zoey/dev/worktrees/content-plan-v2-input --out . --check
```

`--content` must be a clean checkout of `atrinik/content` at `3828651987a5` (the shared checkout was behind `main`, so this run used a separate clone at the current `main`). The scripts need only the Python standard library (the PNGs are written by `raster.py`). The content checkout must be clean under `arch/` and `maps/` (`--allow-dirty` is for experiments and marks the JSON). Artifacts are generated in a staging directory, the staged JSON is verified again, and only then are `placement-plan.json`, `REPORT.md` and the PNGs replaced; a failed run leaves the old artifacts in place. A group that cannot be placed stays unplaced and fails verification, so such a plan is never published. `test_placement_plan.py` tampers with copies of the committed JSON and checks that the verifier rejects each one; it needs no content checkout. `verify_plan.py` makes two checks. Without `--content` it re-derives the placement rules from the JSON alone. With `--content` it also rebuilds the plan from the content with the generator and requires the published JSON to equal the rebuild field for field (the checkout path aside), so an edit that keeps the JSON consistent with itself (an exit dropped from `exit_inventory` together with its count, say) fails there. `build_plan.py --check` regenerates every artifact in a temporary directory and fails unless each is byte-identical (run it with the `--content` path the plan records).

The run is deterministic (sorted inputs, lexicographic tie-breaks) and read-only against the content checkout; rerunning with the same arguments on the same content rewrites `placement-plan.json`, `REPORT.md` and the PNGs byte-for-byte. `content_git_head`, `content_worktree_clean` and `content_inputs_digest` (SHA-256 over every file under `arch/` and `maps/`, checked again after the run) in the JSON identify the input. Scripts: `mapdata.py` (read-only reader, tile links, occupied tiles, floors per sub-layer, `no_pass` tiles), `analysis.py` (components, layout, exit resolution), `relayer.py` (the relayering: regions, units, conflicts, seams), `footprint.py` (gap rule, column and sloped stairwell chains), `build_plan.py` (classification, relayering, placement, stairwells, wiring, JSON, staged publication), `report.py`, `render.py` and `raster.py` (images), and `verify_plan.py` (independent check of the JSON). Tunables (`VIEW_RADIUS`, `CONTACT_MARGIN`, `MAX_EXTRA_LAYERS`, `MAX_SHIFT`, `STAIR_TOLERANCE`, `STAIRWELL_MAX_BEND`, `LAYER_HEIGHT_Z` in `build_plan.py`; `MIN_REGION_TILES`, `WALKABLE_DZ`, `SEAM_CELL` in `relayer.py`) and the hand-written inputs (system maps, the owner's decisions of 2026-10-18 and 2026-10-19 with their dates, version 1's placements for the comparison) are constants.

`mapdata.py` is a read-only reader for this analysis, not the toolkit's parser. At content `b9657299a6e7` it was cross-checked against the canonical `atrinik_source::Document::parse` records (with `atrinik-catalog`'s rule that an `arch` field opens an embedded object and `end` closes it): all 3629 map files (2,058,974 top-level objects) and 1358 archetype files (3,805 objects) agree on every object name, field value and child count, with 0 differences.
